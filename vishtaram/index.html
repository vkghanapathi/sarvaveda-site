Veda Vijñāna Viṣṭaram

वेद विज्ञान विष्टरम् — चतुर्वेद विद्यालयः

An institution of eminence for the study of Veda, Vedāṅga and Vedic philosophy. All four Vedas are taught in the traditional gurukula manner, with their own śākhās, their Brāhmaṇas and Āraṇyakas, and the full complement of recitation methods up to Ghana Pāṭha. Viṣṭaram is the teaching and publishing body whose 72-volume Series forms the training spine of the SarvaVeda model.

Chaturveda Vidyālaya

The four Veda schools

Each school teaches its own śākhā from the Saṃhitā through the Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka, together with the six Vedāṅgas, and carries the student through the vikṛti recitation methods — Krama, Jaṭā and Ghana.

Ṛgvedaऋग्वेद

Śākala Śākhā

The Śākala Saṃhitā — 64 adhyāyas arranged as 8 aṣṭakas across 10 maṇḍalas — with the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa in 40 adhyāyas, and Pada Pāṭha through Krama, Jaṭā and Ghana Pāṭha.

Faculty: Sri Avadhanula Chinmaya Datta Ghanapathi, assisted by Dr. Vamshi Krishna Ghanapathi  ·  Duration: 14 years — textual study (1–6), recitation methods (7–10), philosophical commentary (11–14)

Yajurvedaयजुर्वेद

Taittirīya Śākhā

The Taittirīya Saṃhitā with its Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka, and the Vedāṅga complement — śikṣā, vyākaraṇa, chandas and the ritual sūtras.

Faculty: Sri Dwibhashyam Hanumat Prasanna Ghanapathi (principal), assisted by Dr. Vamshi Krishna Ghanapathi  ·  Duration: 14 years, covering text, auxiliaries and ritual application

Sāmavedaसामवेद

Kauthuma Śākhā

The Sāmaveda Saṃhitā — 1,875 mantras across the Pūrvārcika and Uttarārcika — with the Chāndogya Upaniṣad, and a systematic progression through the gāna traditions: Āraṇyaka Gāna, the Ūha variations, and the Brāhmaṇas.

Faculty: Sri K. Chaitanya Kumar Shrouti, assisted by Dr. Vamshi Krishna Ghanapathi  ·  Duration: 10 years, with a documented 14-year advanced pathway

Atharvavedaअथर्ववेद

Śaunaka & Paippalāda

The Atharvaveda in 20 kāṇḍas, with the Gopatha Brāhmaṇa and the Upaniṣads, alongside Pāṇini's grammar, Piṅgala's metrics and Yāska's etymology.

Faculty: Sri Kadiyal Suryanarayana Sharma (head), with Sri Vanshikrishna Ghanapathi and Sri Omkar Ramdasi  ·  Duration: 12-year integrated programme
Vedāṅga & applied śāstra

Eight further schools

The auxiliary disciplines that make the Veda intelligible, and the applied traditions that put it to use in ritual and daily observance.

Vyākaraṇa

Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī, Patañjali's Mahābhāṣya, and the Vaiyākaraṇa Siddhānta Kaumudī with the Laghu Siddhānta Kaumudī.

Topics: sandhi, samāsa, pratyaya, kāraka, dhātu, subanta, tiṅanta

Jyotiṣa

Vedic astronomy, calendar systems and the astrological sciences, from Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa through the Siddhānta and Saṃhitā traditions.

Texts: Bṛhat Saṃhitā, Sūrya Siddhānta  ·  Topics: Horā Śāstra, Pañcāṅga Gaṇita, Muhūrta, Praśna

Śrauta

The theory and practice of the major Vedic sacrifices according to the Śrauta Sūtra traditions.

Texts: Āśvalāyana and Āpastamba Śrauta Sūtras; Agnihotra, the Soma yāgas, and the seasonal sacrifices

Smārta Prayoga

The practical application of Vedic ritual in daily worship, life-cycle ceremony and household observance.

Topics: nitya karma, sandhyā vandana, gṛhya prayoga, the ṣoḍaśa saṃskāras, vrata and utsava vidhi, pūjā vidhāna

Nitya Archana

Deity worship and temple ritual in the Āgama traditions — installation, daily service, and festival celebration.

Topics: ritual procedure, mantra recitation, mudrā, offerings, and temple management across eight practical domains

Saṃskṛtam

Sanskrit from the ground up: basic prayers, speech production and syllabary, word forms, verb conjugation, numerals, prefixes, and sentence comprehension.

Enrolment: by phone or email — see contact

Svadharma

Taught as a paired discipline alongside Sāmaveda and Atharvaveda, on one's own duty and observance within the tradition.

Faculty: Sri K. Chaitanya Kumar Shrouty, Sri Omkar Ramdasi

Nyāya

The science of reasoning and valid cognition — the pramāṇa framework and formal inference that the Chatur-Darśana engine of SarvaVeda models computationally.

The gurukula day

Eleven hours of study, from five in the morning

The residential day opens with āvṛtti at 5:00 AM and closes at 9:00 PM, structured around recitation, with breaks for sandhyā, meals and personal development. Modern subjects are taught alongside, so that a student leaves with both the tradition and a recognised degree.
